Who scores, not what to bet

Expected result -$5.26 (-17.0%) against -$2.14 (-6.9%) for the same $31.00 on the 5 singles alone. A cover is leverage on the edge in each leg: per dollar a single returns e and a k-fold returns (1+e)^k − 1. These prices leave a negative edge, so multiplying them pays the margin once per leg and the cover loses harder than the singles do. Only a better price or a better probability fixes that; the structure cannot.

Graded. Replayed over 66 weeks (2023-2025) at $1.00 a slip on the prices actually posted, this rule returned between -22.4% and -1.2% depending on pool size, and no pool size had an interval clearing zero. 5 of 6 returned less than the same money on the singles — which is what a negative edge per leg does when it is multiplied: a k-fold pays the margin k times. Would a different rule have helped? Over the same 66 weeks, ranking by the model's own number instead of the blended one returned +19.0% at 4 players against -12.9% — ahead at 4 of 6 sizes. A cover is leverage on the edge in each leg: per dollar a single returns e and a k-fold returns (1+e)^k − 1.
