Expected result -$5.25 (-16.9%) against -$2.10 (-6.8%) for the same $31.00 on the 5 singles alone. A cover is leverage on the edge in each leg: per dollar a single returns e and a k-fold returns (1+e)^k − 1. These prices leave a negative edge, so multiplying them pays the margin once per leg and the cover loses harder than the singles do. Only a better price or a better probability fixes that; the structure cannot.

Graded. Replayed over 66 weeks (2023-2025) at $1.00 a slip on the prices actually posted, this rule returned between -34.2% and +2.4% depending on pool size, and no pool size had an interval clearing zero. 5 of 6 returned less than the same money on the singles — which is what a negative edge per leg does when it is multiplied: a k-fold pays the margin k times. At the 5 you have picked it returned -5.5% [-26%, 18%] with 21 of 57 weeks ahead.

Would a different rule have helped? Over the same 66 weeks, ranking by the model's own number instead of the blended one returned +9.5% at 3 players against +2.4% — ahead at 3 of 6 sizes. No interval clears zero, and the best of 44 random orderings reached +123% on the same weeks — so read this as a direction, not a result. Blending pulls our number toward the price, which is what makes it better calibrated and also what deletes the disagreement a bet lives on.
