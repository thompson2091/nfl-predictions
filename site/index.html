Who scores, not what to bet

This board ranks players by how likely they are to score. Its top pick is right 51.6% of the time against 16.2% for a candidate drawn at random — about 3.2x the field, over 849 backtested games (2023-2025). Restricted to picks it rates above 0.50 it reaches 60.9% [56.7%, 64.8%], on 45% of games.

Against the bookmaker, same 849 games (2023-2025). The honest yardstick — a price, not a coin flip.

Only the backable half is a bet. A cover multiplies whatever edge each leg carries, so it multiplies that half — not the average of the two, which is higher because the fade side is sharper.

Breakeven is 4 of 5 — and that happens 17.3% of the time, with the measured teammate correlation of -0.024 applied.

Returns vary within a row because the legs are priced differently — two of five can be the two short legs or the two long ones. The figure shown is weighted by how likely each combination is.

Expected result -$5.40 (-17.4%) against -$2.00 (-6.5%) for the same $31.00 on the 5 singles alone. A cover is leverage on the edge in each leg: per dollar a single returns e and a k-fold returns (1+e)^k − 1. These prices leave a negative edge, so multiplying them pays the margin once per leg and the cover loses harder than the singles do. Only a better price or a better probability fixes that; the structure cannot.

Graded. Replayed over 66 weeks (2023-2025) at $1.00 a slip on the prices actually posted, this rule returned between -22.4% and -1.2% depending on pool size, and no pool size had an interval clearing zero. 5 of 6 returned less than the same money on the singles — which is what a negative edge per leg does when it is multiplied: a k-fold pays the margin k times. At the 5 you have picked it returned -10.6% [-30%, 11%] with 19 of 57 weeks ahead.

Would a different rule have helped? Over the same 66 weeks, ranking by the model's own number instead of the blended one returned +19.0% at 4 players against -12.9% — ahead at 4 of 6 sizes. No interval clears zero, and the best of 44 random orderings reached +274% on the same weeks — so read this as a direction, not a result. Blending pulls our number toward the price, which is what makes it better calibrated and also what deletes the disagreement a bet lives on.

Expected result -$19.16 (-61.8%) against -$8.97 (-28.9%) for the same $31.00 on the 5 singles alone. A cover is leverage on the edge in each leg: per dollar a single returns e and a k-fold returns (1+e)^k − 1. These prices leave a negative edge, so multiplying them pays the margin once per leg and the cover loses harder than the singles do. Only a better price or a better probability fixes that; the structure cannot.

Graded. Replayed over 66 weeks (2023-2025) at $1.00 a slip on the prices actually posted, this rule returned between -66.9% and +52.7% depending on pool size, and no pool size had an interval clearing zero. 5 of 6 returned less than the same money on the singles — which is what a negative edge per leg does when it is multiplied: a k-fold pays the margin k times. At the 5 you have picked it returned -31.1% [-56%, -2%] with 16 of 57 weeks ahead.

Would a different rule have helped? Not by re-ranking: this market's blend does not move the number, so the model's own ordering and the published one pick the same players at every size. Of 44 rules tried over 66 weeks, none has an interval clearing zero.
